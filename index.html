Abstract

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has achieved remarkable success in novel view synthesis. However, 3DGS severely overfits to training views in the sparse-view setting due to insufficient constraints, leading to the creation of floating artifacts that are inconsistent with the correct geometry, and results in blurry backgrounds with significant detail loss in novel views. Our analysis reveals that this degradation arises not only from the lack of geometric regularization under photometric-only supervision, but also from the significant overlap between the opacity distributions of floating artifacts and valid scene Gaussians, which makes opacity-based pruning ineffective in distinguishing between them. To address these challenges, we propose DuoReg-GS, an asymmetric dual-regularized framework that is based on two critical designs. First, we develop Asymmetric Dual-Model Regularization to train two parallel 3DGS models with different densification and pruning strategies, enforcing cross-model depth consistency and geometric filtering to suppress artifacts while preserving structural fidelity. Second, we introduce Opacity Perturbation Regularization to periodically reset and constrain the opacity during training, decoupling floating artifacts from valid scene Gaussians in the opacity distribution and promoting view-consistent appearance learning. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance in the sparse-view settings of standard benchmark datasets. Our code will be made publicly available.

Key Motivation

We identify that the Gaussian densification process, while enhancing scene detail capture, unintentionally contributes to these artifacts by growing additional Gaussians that model transient disturbances.

Analysis of Gaussian densification in transient object fitting

As training progresses, vanilla 3DGS suffers from performance degradation and exhibits artifacts due to the increasing number of Gaussians. Disabling Gaussian densification notably improves the results, even achieving performance comparable to the recent robust method SpotLessSplats. Despite producing transient-free rendering, 3DGS w/o densification struggles to recover fine details in regions with sparse Gaussian initialization (highlighted by red arrows).

Methodology

Overview of the proposed DuoReg-GS. Given a set of sparse input views, DuoReg-GS trains two 3DGS models with asymmetric densification, which amplify the spatial discrepancies between valid scene Gaussians and floating artifacts. These artifacts are then suppressed using a depth-alignment loss within each model and are removed through collaborative geometric filtering across the two models. To further decouple artifacts from true scene geometry, Opacity Perturbation Regularization (OPR) randomizes Gaussian opacity at each iteration, leading to a progressive decay in the opacity of artifacts and enabling their removal via the native opacity-based pruning in 3DGS. After training, the primary model is used for novel view synthesis.
